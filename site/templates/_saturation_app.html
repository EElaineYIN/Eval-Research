How each benchmark is classified

One label per benchmark, from two questions: is the top score close to the ceiling, and can the top models still be told apart?

Close to ceilingFar from ceiling
Top 5 not separableSaturatedNo room left and no signal between leaders.Under-poweredScores are still low, but the test cannot separate the leaders (often too few items).
Top 5 separableNear ceilingClose to the top, but leaders still differ. Watch it.DiscriminativeRoom to grow and real gaps between models.
With item count n (primary): "not separable" means S ≥ threshold, where S = exp(−R²), R = (s₁ − s₅) ÷ √[(s₁(1−s₁) + s₅(1−s₅)) / n_eff], n_eff = nα (Akhtar et al., ICML 2026). α = 1 treats items as independent; α = 0.5 assumes heavy correlation. Default α = 0.8.
Without n (fallback, marked ◆): "not separable" means the gap between 1st and 5th ≤ a fixed number of points.
Close to ceiling in both cases: headroom = 100 − top score ≤ a fixed number of points.

Caveats

  • Single snapshot (Oct 15, 2026). No history yet, so the pace of progress is not measured.
  • BenchLM does not say whether a score is self-reported or independently reproduced; leaders may be optimistic.
  • Some entries (e.g. τ²-Bench) mix several domains or metrics in one number, so their saturation reading is noisy.
  • Item counts come from original papers, official pages or BenchLM's own descriptions. "BenchLM text" and "Inferred from base" are not individually checked. The formula assumes the top 5 were scored on the same items, which is not guaranteed.
  • α = 0.8 has no theoretical basis; it sits between the paper's default (0.5) and independent items (1). Labels marked "Varies" are sensitive to that choice.
  • Scores use each model family's best entry. BenchLM lists 161 agentic entries; 108 have scores.
